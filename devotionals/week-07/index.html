Mary’s Song: Mary Explains the Moment

Luke 1:39–56

Before You Begin

These commentaries grow out of this week’s passage. They are not required, and they are not the main thing. They are simply here to help, a little extra for your study. Feel free to use them or not, and to choose the point that best fits your group’s conversation. You do not need to cover everything. What matters most is to read the passage, pray, study it together and let it reach your hearts, so that we respond to God in obedience to his Word. Each point also opens other themes you can explore, as your time and energy allow. Our prayer is that God will guide you, give you wisdom and bring fruit from your time in his Word.

Only the Bible is inspired by God. These commentaries are human reflections, always read them under the authority of God’s Word.

Last week, Mary said yes to God's word. Now she hurries to see Elizabeth, the one person who could understand what God was doing. Elizabeth speaks a blessing, and Mary answers with a song that explains the moment. These three reflections follow the passage: a safe place to share what God is doing, a song shaped by God's Word, and a faith that sings before it sees.

A Safe Place to Share What God Is Doing

Luke 1:39 to 45 · Luke 1:56

Mary "went into the hill country with haste" (Luke 1:39). She was young, engaged and pregnant, and people in Nazareth would not understand. Even Joseph first planned to end the engagement quietly (Matthew 1:19). So Mary went to the person God had already prepared, her relative Elizabeth.

Elizabeth was older, the wife of a priest, and she had waited many years for her own child. She could have felt that Mary's news was bigger than hers. Instead, "filled with the Holy Spirit," she blessed her: "Why am I so favored, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?" (Luke 1:41, 43). In Luke's Gospel, she is the first person to call Jesus "Lord."

Before anyone else believed Mary's story, God gave her a home where she was welcomed and encouraged. Mary "stayed with her about three months" (Luke 1:56). Faith often grows in friendships like this, where our story is received with joy instead of suspicion.

Reflection question

God gave Mary an Elizabeth, someone who welcomed her story with joy. How can we, by God's grace, become that kind of safe and joyful place for others in our group and in our church?

Optional Ideas to Explore

- Luke 15:6 · "Rejoice with me." When the shepherd finds his lost sheep, "he calls together his friends and his neighbors, saying to them, 'Rejoice with me.'" Joy grows when it is shared. God made us to celebrate together.

- 1 Thessalonians 5:11 · Building each other up. "Therefore exhort one another, and build each other up, even as you also do." Encouragement is not a small thing. It is one of the ways God strengthens faith.

A Song Shaped by God’s Word

Luke 1:46 to 50 · Luke 24:27, 32

"My soul magnifies the Lord. My spirit has rejoiced in God my Savior" (Luke 1:46 to 47). To magnify is to make something look bigger. Mary does not make herself bigger. She makes God bigger in the eyes of those who hear her. And she calls him "my Savior," because she too needed his mercy.

Almost every line of her song echoes the Old Testament, the song of Hannah, the Psalms and the promises to Abraham. Mary came from a small town, but her heart was full of Scripture. So when something huge and confusing happened to her, she had words to understand it.

That is what this week's title means: Mary explains the moment. She read her experience through God's Word, not God's Word through her experience. When life brings news we did not expect, the Scripture stored in our hearts helps us see where God is. After the resurrection, Jesus did this for two confused disciples, and their hearts were "burning" as he opened the Scriptures (Luke 24:27, 32).

Reflection question

Mary's heart was so full of Scripture that she could understand her own story through it. How can God's Word, stored up in our hearts, help us see God's hand in the unexpected moments of our lives?

Optional Ideas to Explore

- Psalm 119:105 · A lamp for the next step. "Your word is a lamp to my feet, and a light for my path." A lamp does not show the whole road at once. It shows enough to take the next step with God.

- Deuteronomy 6:6 to 7 · Words that live in the home. "These words, which I command you today, shall be on your heart," and "you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way." Mary probably learned many of these words at home, long before she needed them.

A Faith That Sings Before It Sees

Luke 1:51 to 55 · Romans 4:17 to 21

Listen to the verbs in the second half of the song: "He has put down princes from their thrones, and has exalted the lowly. He has filled the hungry with good things" (Luke 1:52 to 53). Mary speaks as if all this has already happened.

But Caesar still ruled in Rome, Herod still sat on his throne, and the poor were still hungry. Mary had no power at all, and Jesus was not even born yet. Why sing in the past tense? Because when God has promised something, it is as good as done.

Her reason is God's faithfulness: he remembers mercy, "as he spoke to our fathers, to Abraham and his offspring forever" (Luke 1:55). Mary believed the child inside her was God keeping that ancient promise. Abraham trusted the same God, who "calls the things that are not, as though they were" (Romans 4:17).

This is not wishful thinking. It is trust in a God who has never broken his word. We can sing in the waiting too, because we know who has promised.

Reflection question

Mary praised God for things she could not yet see, because she trusted his promises. How can God's faithfulness in the past give us courage to praise him in the seasons when we are still waiting?

Optional Ideas to Explore

- Romans 4:18 to 20 · Hope against hope. "Against hope, Abraham in hope believed." He looked at his old body and at Sarah, and yet "he didn't waver through unbelief, but grew strong through faith, giving glory to God." Giving glory to God was part of how his faith grew.

- Acts 16:25 · Singing at midnight. In a prison in Philippi, with their backs wounded, "Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them." Praise in dark places is a strong witness to the people around us.
